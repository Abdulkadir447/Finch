Chapter 8 — Orders Schema
8.1 Purpose

The Orders module is the heart of Finch. It manages the complete sales lifecycle, from creating quotations and draft orders to payment, invoicing, fulfillment, returns, and reporting.

The Orders module is tightly integrated with:

Customers
Inventory
Payments
AI Assistant
Reports
Notifications
Synchronization
Audit Logs

Every order belongs to exactly one registered business.

8.2 Objectives

The Orders module must:

Support draft orders
Support quotations
Support completed sales
Support partial payments
Support discounts
Support taxes
Support inventory deduction
Support AI-generated orders
Support returns
Support cancellations
Support refunds
Work offline
Synchronize automatically

8.29 Developer Implementation Notes

Implementation guidelines:

Use UUIDs for all order-related tables.
Store product snapshots to preserve historical accuracy.
Keep discounts, taxes, refunds, and returns in separate normalized tables.
Deduct inventory only after successful payment.
Always create AI-generated orders as drafts requiring user confirmation before saving.
Record every status transition in order_status_history.
Support offline creation with version tracking and conflict resolution during synchronization.
Protect all order data with Row Level Security (RLS).
8.30 Acceptance Criteria

The Orders Schema is complete when:

Orders support the full lifecycle from draft to completion.
Quotes, returns, refunds, and exchanges are supported.
Multiple discounts and taxes can be applied.
Inventory updates occur only after payment.
AI can generate draft orders.
Product snapshots preserve historical accuracy.
Offline order processing functions correctly.
Synchronization uploads only modified records.
All actions are audited and protected by RLS.
8.31 Chapter Summary

The Orders Schema provides the transactional backbone of Finch. It supports a complete order lifecycle, integrates seamlessly with inventory, payments, customers, AI, and reporting, and maintains historical accuracy through product snapshots and status history. Its offline-first design, normalized structure, and security model ensure reliable performance today while supporting future enhancements such as additional payment methods, delivery workflows, and advanced order automation.
